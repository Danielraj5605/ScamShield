        How it works

        Three steps, a few milliseconds

        No account, no setup. You paste a message and read the result.

        1. 01

          Paste the message

          A text or WhatsApp message, or one of the examples. It is held in memory and discarded.

        2. 02

          Four checks run side by side

          Two wording models, a link model that judges the address only, and 23 signals for known scam patterns.

        3. 03

          Get the verdict and next steps

          A risk level, the reasons in plain words, your message with the risky words marked, and what to do.

        Trust

        Private by design

        • Not stored. The message is held in memory and discarded.
        • Minimal logs. Only length, risk level and timing are recorded.
        • Links never opened. A link is judged by its address.
        • Nothing from outside. The site loads no outside fonts, trackers or ads.

        Scam types

        Scams ScamShield recognises

        Each one lists what it usually looks like and what to do. It also recognises lottery and prize, instant loan, investment and fake bank alert scams.

        Fake KYC or account block

        Looks likeA message says your account, card or SIM will be blocked unless you update KYC through a link.

        Do thisBanks do not do this by SMS. Open your bank's official app or visit a branch.

        Requests for your OTP or PIN

        Looks likeSomeone asks you to read out or forward a one-time password, PIN or CVV.

        Do thisNever share these, even with someone who says they are from your bank.

        Fake refund or UPI request

        Looks likeYou are told to approve a request or enter your UPI PIN in order to receive money.

        Do thisYou never need a PIN to receive money. Decline the request.

        Parcel and delivery fees

        Looks likeA parcel is said to be held, and a small fee or address update is requested through a link.

        Do thisCheck orders only in the official courier or shopping app.

        Job and task offers

        Looks likeEasy daily earnings for liking videos or doing tasks, often with a fee to join.

        Do thisReal employers do not charge you to hire you. Do not pay or share documents.

        Electricity or connection cut-off

        Looks likeA threat that power will be cut tonight unless you call a number or pay at once.

        Do thisCheck your bill in the official app or on the electricity board's website.

        Police or official impersonation

        Looks likeA message or caller says a case is registered against you and keeps you on a video call, called "digital arrest".

        Do thisHang up. Officials do not arrest anyone or demand money over a message or call.

        Remote-access apps

        Looks likeYou are asked to install an app such as AnyDesk, or an APK file, to "fix" a problem.

        Do thisNever install it. If you already did, uninstall it and call your bank on the number on your card.

        Questions

        Frequently asked

        Is my message stored?

        No. It is held in memory while the result is made, then discarded. The server log keeps only the length of the message, the risk level, the score and the timing. The privacy policy lists everything.

        Does ScamShield open the links in my message?

        No. It judges a link by its address only, and shows links in a form that cannot be clicked.

        Can it be wrong?

        Yes, in both directions. It can rate a genuine message as risky, and it can miss a new kind of scam. That is why it gives advice and reasons, not orders. If you are unsure, contact the organisation using the number on your card or in its official app.

        Which languages does it work in?

        English only for now. Regional languages are a planned next step.

        Why does it run on an IBM mainframe?

        The realistic use is a bank or telecom checking messages next to its own systems, without sending them to an outside cloud. We trained and ran the whole project on an IBM LinuxONE server to show that this works, and we publish the measurements.

        Who built it?

        A student team, for the IBM Z Datathon 2026. The code and the test results are public on GitHub.
