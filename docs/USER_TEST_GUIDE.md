# Real-user test guide

Goal: find out, honestly, whether real people understand ScamShield's result and what they would do next. Five to ten people is enough. Older relatives and people who are not comfortable with technology are the most useful, because they are the people the tool is for.

## Before you start

- Tell each person what you are doing, that it is a test of the tool and not of them, and that they can stop at any time.
- Use messages you provide (below). Do not ask anyone to share their private messages. If someone chooses to try one of their own, that is their decision, and you must not copy it down.
- Do not record names, phone numbers or photos. Write initials or "P1, P2" and an age band such as 60 to 70.
- Get permission before you write down or quote anything they say.

## Setup

Open the site on the device the person normally uses, if possible a phone. For the demo server, open the tunnel on your laptop and let them use your laptop, or show the recorded demo. Do not help during the tasks. If they are stuck, wait, and write down where.

## The session (about 10 minutes)

1. Ask: "Have you ever received a message you were not sure about? What did you do?" Write down the answer.
2. Give three tasks, one at a time. Use the example cards on the home page:
   - A scam (for example **Fake KYC alert**).
   - A genuine bank message (**Genuine bank OTP** or **Genuine debit alert**).
   - A harder case (**Message from a relative** or **Parcel fee**).
3. For each, ask the person to paste or choose the message, check it, and then say in their own words what the result means and what they would do. Do not read the result to them.
4. Afterwards ask: What was clear? What was confusing? Did the advice make sense? Would you use this? What is missing?

## What to record

| Participant | Age band | Device | Task | Understood the verdict without help (yes / no) | What they said they would do | Exact words (with permission) | Where they hesitated |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## How to report it

Report counts and quotes, not impressions. For example: "Of 7 people, 5 understood the result without help on the scam message, and 4 knew what to do next." State how many people took part and how you chose them. If the number is small, say so. Quote people only with permission and without names. Include the problems you found and what you changed, because a test that finds nothing is less believable than one that found something and fixed it.

Never invent a participant, a quote or a number, and do not round up. A short honest result is worth more than a long polished one.
